⚡ Understanding Voltage & Cell Direction

Explore how voltage changes when you connect 1.5 V cells in series—either pointing in the same direction or in the opposite direction—using a virtual voltmeter.

🔋 What is Voltage?

Voltage (also called potential difference) is the electrical "push" that drives current around a circuit. It is measured in Volts (V). One standard cell provides 1.5 V.

🎛️ Using a Voltmeter

A voltmeter measures the size of the voltage between two points. It is always connected in parallel across the cells, with the red lead (+) and black lead (−).

🔄 Why Direction Matters

Cells pointing the same way push together, so their voltages add up. Cells pointing the opposite way push against each other, so their voltages cancel out (subtract).

Key Rule: The size of the total voltage equals the number of forward cells minus the number of reversed cells, multiplied by 1.5 V. A negative sign (−) on a digital voltmeter simply means the net push is in the opposite direction!
